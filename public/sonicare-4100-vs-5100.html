Philips Sonicare 4100 vs 5100 (2026): The Deciding Differences

The Philips Sonicare 4100 and 5100 are the two most popular entry-level Sonicare models, and they are frequently confused. The 4100 typically sells for $29–$34 on sale; the 5100 runs $49–$59. Both share the same sonic motor, the same 14-day battery life, and the same fundamental brushing technology. The differences are in features — and deciding which features matter to you is the entire decision.

Who Should Pick the Sonicare 4100

The 4100 is the right choice if you are budget-focused, switching from a manual toothbrush for the first time, or certain that you will use one cleaning mode and never want more. It delivers the same fundamental brushing experience as the 5100 — same motor, same sonic technology, same timer — for $20 less. If you do not have a history of brushing too hard and do not need gum care coaching, the 4100 is simply the better value.

Who Should Pick the Sonicare 5100

Choose the 5100 if: you have been told by a dentist that you brush too hard (the pressure sensor alerts you before damage occurs), you want a dedicated Gum Care mode for periodontal health, you want BrushSync head tracking to remind you when to replace your brush head, or you simply want more flexibility in your routine. The $20 premium for the 5100 is one of the best value upgrades in the Sonicare lineup.

Gum Care Mode: Is It Real?

The 5100's Gum Care mode runs at a reduced intensity with a pulse pattern designed to massage gum tissue and stimulate blood flow at the gumline. Clinical evidence on the benefits of gum massage modes is mixed, but for people with early gingivitis or gum sensitivity, having a softer setting specifically oriented toward gum tissue is useful. The 4100's single Clean mode runs at full intensity only, with no ability to dial down for gum work.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the Sonicare 5100 worth $20 more than the 4100?

It depends on what you will use. The 5100 adds two extra modes (White and Gum Care), a pressure sensor, and BrushSync brush head tracking. If you want gum health coaching, a pressure alert, or you switch modes regularly, the 5100 is worth $20 more. If you plan to use one mode and never change heads programmatically, the 4100 is the smarter buy.

Does the Sonicare 4100 have a pressure sensor?

No. The Sonicare 4100 does not include a pressure sensor. The ProtectiveClean 5100 is the lowest-priced Sonicare model to include the pressure sensor. If brushing pressure is a concern — you have been told you brush too hard — the 5100 or a higher model is the right choice.

What does BrushSync do on the Sonicare 5100?

BrushSync is a system where Sonicare brush heads with a colored indicator ring communicate with compatible handles to track brush head age. The handle reminds you when to replace the head, typically after 3 months of use. The 4100 does not have BrushSync and will work with any Sonicare head but will not track wear automatically.
