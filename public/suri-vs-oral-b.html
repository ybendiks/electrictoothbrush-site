SURI 2.0 wins on sustainability, battery life, and travel design. Oral-B iO Series 4 wins on cleaning technology, pressure guidance, and replacement head availability. Which is better depends entirely on what you prioritise.

Cleaning Technology

SURI uses sonic vibration at 31,000 strokes per minute — the same category as Philips Sonicare. Oral-B uses its iO magnetic drive system, which combines oscillating-rotating motion with micro-vibrations. Independent clinical studies have found both technologies remove plaque more effectively than manual brushing, with Oral-B's round head claiming stronger results on proximal surfaces (between teeth).

For most users the difference in real-world plaque removal is marginal. Technique, brushing time, and consistency matter far more than the technology type.

Sustainability

SURI is a certified B Corporation with compostable packaging, plant-based bristles, and a mail-back head recycling programme. Oral-B does not hold B Corp status and sells conventional replacement heads. If reducing plastic waste is important to you, SURI is the clear choice.

Battery and Travel

SURI's 38-day battery on USB-C is outstanding. For frequent travellers this alone can justify the higher price — you can charge with the same cable as your phone and go a month without thinking about it. Oral-B's 14-day battery and inductive charger mean you need a specific charger or travel with the stand.

Which Should You Buy?

Choose SURI 2.0 if: sustainability matters, you travel frequently, you want USB-C charging, or you prefer a minimalist two-mode design.
Choose Oral-B iO Series 4 if: you want five cleaning modes, a more explicit LED pressure sensor, a lower price, or the wider range of replacement head options.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is SURI better than Oral-B?

SURI is better for sustainability and travel; Oral-B is better for pressure guidance and mode variety. Neither is objectively superior — it depends on your priorities.

Does SURI have a pressure sensor?

SURI changes its vibration pattern when you brush too hard, but there is no explicit LED or app alert. Oral-B's LED ring gives a clearer green/amber/red signal.
