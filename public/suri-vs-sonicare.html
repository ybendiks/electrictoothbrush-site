The Short Answer

Both SURI and Sonicare are sonic toothbrushes using the same underlying vibration technology. SURI wins on sustainability and battery. Sonicare wins on price (4100), mode variety (5700+), and replacement head ecosystem. At the same price point the Sonicare 4100 at $49 is the better pure value; the SURI 2.0 at $134.99 is for buyers who want eco-credentials and the UV-C travel case.

Cleaning Power: Is There a Difference?

Both use sonic vibration at 31,000 strokes per minute. Cleaning power at this speed is effectively identical between brands. What varies is head design, bristle pattern, and mode. Neither SURI nor the Sonicare 4100 will clean meaningfully better than the other in everyday use — technique and time matter far more.

SURI 2.0 vs Sonicare 5700 (Same Price Tier)

At $79.99, the Sonicare 5700 undercuts SURI on price by $55. The 5700 adds three cleaning modes and intensity levels but has a shorter 21-day battery and no UV-C case. SURI is still the eco pick; Sonicare is the features-per-dollar pick.

Who Should Buy SURI

Buy the SURI 2.0 if: you travel frequently and value USB-C charging, sustainability certifications matter to you, you want a UV-C sanitising case, or you prefer a minimal two-mode design from a B Corp brand.

Who Should Buy Sonicare

Buy Sonicare if: value is your priority (4100 at $49), you want multiple modes and intensities (5700+), you use Sonicare brush heads already, or you want the most advanced sonic flagship (9900 Prestige Next-Gen).

Frequently Asked Questions

Is SURI as good as Sonicare?

For plaque removal, yes — both use 31,000 strokes-per-minute sonic technology. SURI differentiates on sustainability and travel design; Sonicare differentiates on value, mode variety, and head selection.

Which lasts longer, SURI or Sonicare?

SURI 2.0 lasts 38 days per charge; Sonicare 4100 lasts 14 days. SURI wins decisively on battery. However, Sonicare handles typically last 5–7 years before showing performance decline, similar to SURI's rated handle lifespan.
