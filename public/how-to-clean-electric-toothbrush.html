How to Clean an Electric Toothbrush (Step-by-Step)

Electric toothbrushes live in a warm, humid bathroom environment and come into contact with bacteria, toothpaste residue, and food particles every day. Without regular cleaning, the connection joint between handle and head can develop mold, and the handle can accumulate grime that becomes a bacteria source. Here is exactly how to keep yours clean.

After Every Brush: The 30-Second Routine

This takes under 30 seconds and removes the majority of bacteria and residue before it can dry and become harder to remove.

Step 1: Rinse the brush head. With the brush still running, hold it under warm running water for 5–10 seconds to flush toothpaste out of the bristles. Then press the power button to stop and shake off excess water.

Step 2: Wipe the handle. Use a dry or slightly damp cloth to wipe the handle, especially around the power button where toothpaste accumulates.

Step 3: Store upright. Stand the brush upright in an open-air holder that allows airflow around the head. Never store a wet brush in a closed case — the trapped moisture is where mold grows.

Weekly Cleaning

Step 1: Remove the brush head. Pull it straight off the handle spindle. Different brands have slightly different attachment mechanisms, but most pull off with a firm straight pull.

Step 2: Soak the head. Fill a glass with antiseptic mouthwash (any brand) or a 1:1 mixture of white vinegar and water. Soak the detached brush head for 20–30 minutes. This kills bacteria and loosens toothpaste buildup in and around the bristle base.

Step 3: Clean the handle. While the head soaks, wipe the handle with a damp cloth. Pay attention to the spindle connection point — use a cotton swab to clean inside the connection socket where residue collects. Do not submerge the handle or run water into the charging port.

Step 4: Rinse and reattach. Rinse the head under warm water, shake off excess moisture, and reattach to the handle. Store upright.

Monthly Deep Clean

Head soak in hydrogen peroxide: For a more thorough monthly clean, soak the detached head in a solution of equal parts 3% hydrogen peroxide and water for 30 minutes. Hydrogen peroxide is more effective than vinegar at killing bacteria and whitening any discoloration on the bristle base.

Connection joint inspection: Use a small mirror and flashlight to inspect the connection joint on both the handle and the head socket. If you see black or gray discoloration, this is mold. Clean it immediately with a cotton swab soaked in hydrogen peroxide.

Charger base cleaning: Wipe down the charging stand with a slightly damp cloth. Toothpaste drips and water spots accumulate on the charger. Check the charging contacts (the inductive coil on Sonicare models, or the pins on Oral-B models) for corrosion or grime. Use a dry cotton swab to clean the contacts if needed.

What to Avoid

  • Dishwasher: Destroys bristles and warps plastic components. Never.
  • Submerging the handle: Most electric toothbrush handles are splash-resistant but not submersible. The charging port and motor housing are not designed to be submerged in water.
  • Harsh chemicals on charging contacts: Bleach or abrasive cleaners can damage the contacts. Stick to vinegar or hydrogen peroxide for cleaning near electronic components.
  • Closed storage when wet: Always let the brush air dry before putting it in a travel case.

When to Replace the Head

Replace your brush head every 3 months, or sooner if the bristles are visibly frayed, discolored, or bent outward. Frayed bristles remove less plaque and can cause minor gum irritation. Many brushes include a color-indicator bristle that fades from blue to white when replacement is due — use this as your primary signal if your brush has it.

UV Sanitizers: Worth It?

UV toothbrush sanitizer cases claim to kill 99.9% of bacteria using UV-C light. The technology works in laboratory conditions, but the practical benefit over regular rinsing and mouthwash soaks is debated. If you have a compromised immune system or simply prefer the peace of mind, a UV sanitizer is a reasonable addition. For most healthy adults, the weekly mouthwash soak achieves comparable cleanliness without the device.

Frequently Asked Questions

How often should I clean my electric toothbrush?

Rinse the brush head after every use and wipe the handle weekly. Do a deep clean monthly: soak the detached head in mouthwash or diluted white vinegar for 30 minutes, wipe down the handle and charging base. Check the connection joint between head and handle for mold buildup monthly.

Can I put my electric toothbrush head in the dishwasher?

No. Dishwasher heat and detergents damage the bristles and the plastic of electric toothbrush heads. The high-temperature environment can warp the bristles and reduce cleaning effectiveness significantly. Stick to manual cleaning with mouthwash, white vinegar solution, or hydrogen peroxide.

How do I get rid of mold on my electric toothbrush?

Mold most commonly grows in the connection joint between the head and handle. Remove the head and soak it in a 1:1 dilution of white vinegar and water for 30 minutes. Use a cotton swab soaked in hydrogen peroxide to clean the joint on the handle. Rinse thoroughly, dry, and reattach. Store your brush upright in an open-air holder, not in a closed case.
