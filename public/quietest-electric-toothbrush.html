Why Noise Matters

Electric toothbrushes range from around 45 dB (barely audible) to 75 dB (similar to a hair dryer). Noise matters for early-morning brushing in shared homes, for children who are noise-sensitive, and for anyone who finds high-frequency vibration sound unpleasant. Frequently Asked Questions

What is the quietest electric toothbrush?

The Quip and Oclean X Ultra both measure around 45 dB. For sonic speed combined with quiet, the Oclean X Ultra is the quietest high-performance option. The Sonicare 9900 Prestige Next-Gen (55 dB) is the quietest flagship from a major brand.

Why are sonic toothbrushes loud?

Sonic brushes vibrate at 31,000–42,000 strokes per minute. The motor and brush head transmit some of that vibration as sound through the handle and into the jaw, amplifying perceived noise. Higher-end motors are better engineered to dampen this transmission.

Does a quieter toothbrush clean less well?

Not necessarily. The Oclean X Ultra is both quieter and faster (42,000 vpm) than many noisier models. Noise is a function of motor engineering and handle materials, not cleaning power.
