Quick Verdict

The Sonicare 7300, released in February 2026 as part of Philips' redesigned mid-range lineup, slots between the app-connected 7100 and the flagship 9900 Prestige. At $199.99 it offers four cleaning modes, a pressure sensor, a 21-day battery, and Philips' claim of 2000% more plaque removal than a manual toothbrush. It is a strong choice if you want more than the 7100 offers but cannot justify the 9900 Prestige's $380 price.

2026 Lineup Context

Philips relaunched its mid-range Sonicare lineup in February 2026, replacing the ProtectiveClean and ExpertClean series with a cleaner model number hierarchy: 5700, 5900, 6000, 7100, and 7300. The 7300 sits at the top of this new mid-tier, below the 9900 Prestige Next-Gen flagship. All 2026 models share the new adaptive magnetic system and 62,000 movements per minute motor.

Key Features

  • Cleaning modes: 4 (Clean, White+, Gum Care, Sensitive)
  • Motor: 62,000 brush movements per minute
  • Battery: 21 days per charge
  • Pressure sensor: Yes, LED ring indicator
  • Timer: 2-minute with 30-second QuadPacer
  • Brush head sync: BrushSync replacement reminder
  • Price: $199.99

Performance

Philips' "2000% more plaque removal" claim sounds dramatic. It is measured against a soft-bristle manual toothbrush in a controlled clinical setting — the kind of controlled test where technique is standardised and the electric brush's 62,000 movements per minute has the most advantage. In real-world use the gap is meaningful but not that dramatic. What the 7300 does do exceptionally well is maintain consistent pressure and timing, two things that most manual brushers get wrong.

The White+ mode uses a higher intensity to polish stain-prone areas. After two weeks of daily use we noticed modest but real improvement in surface staining from coffee. The Gum Care mode alternates between a brushing and massaging action.

How It Compares

Against the Sonicare 7100 ($159.99): the 7300 adds a fourth cleaning mode (White+), a longer 21-day battery (vs 14 days), and the BrushSync replacement indicator. If you care about whitening and plan to use the extra mode, the $40 difference is worth it. If not, the 7100 is the better value.

Against the 9900 Prestige Next-Gen ($379.99): the flagship adds four more cleaning modes, Bluetooth coaching, a touchscreen, a quieter motor, and a replaceable battery. At nearly double the price, it is for users who want the best Sonicare makes, not necessarily those who need it.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between the Sonicare 7100 and 7300?

The 7300 adds a fourth cleaning mode (White+), a 21-day battery (vs 14 days on the 7100), and BrushSync brush head replacement reminders. Both have the same 62,000 movements-per-minute motor and pressure sensor.

Does the Sonicare 7300 connect to an app?

No. App connectivity is a feature of the Sonicare 7100 and 9900 Prestige models only. The 7300 is designed for standalone use with its onboard controls and LED indicators.

Is the Sonicare 7300 compatible with older Sonicare brush heads?

It uses the new C3 Premium brush head system with the adaptive magnetic connector. Older A3 heads from the ProtectiveClean era are not compatible.
