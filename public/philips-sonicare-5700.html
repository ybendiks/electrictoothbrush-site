Quick Verdict

The Sonicare 5700, part of Philips' February 2026 lineup refresh, is the sweet spot in the new Sonicare range. At $79.99 you get two cleaning modes (Clean and Sensitive), three intensity levels, a pressure sensor, a 21-day battery, and the new 62,000-movement-per-minute motor. For most people who want more than the entry-level 4100 but do not need app connectivity, this is the right buy.

2026 Lineup Position

The 5700 replaces the ProtectiveClean 5100 in Philips' lineup. Key upgrades over the old model: the 62,000 movements-per-minute motor (up from 31,000 on ProtectiveClean models), three intensity levels (vs two), and a 21-day battery (vs 14 days). The price has also increased from $69.99 to $79.99 to reflect these improvements.

Performance

The 62,000 movements-per-minute motor is shared across the entire 2026 Sonicare lineup from the 5700 up to the 9900 Prestige Next-Gen. That means the cleaning power is identical to brushes costing four times more — what you are paying for in the pricier models is modes, app features, and conveniences, not a stronger motor.

Three intensity levels give the 5700 meaningful flexibility. Level 1 is gentle enough for post-whitening sensitivity or fresh orthodontic adjustments. Level 3 provides a thorough clean for everyday use. The Sensitive mode at Level 1 is the most gentle combination and suitable for people with exposed dentine.

Sonicare 5700 vs 4100 vs 7100

Against the 4100 ($49): the 5700 adds a second mode (Sensitive), three intensity levels (vs one), and doubles the battery to 21 days. Worth the extra $30 if you want intensity control.

Against the 7100 ($159.99): the 7100 adds two more modes, Bluetooth app coaching, and a four-week battery. If you want app connectivity, pay the extra $80. If not, the 5700 is the smarter buy.

Frequently Asked Questions

What replaced the Sonicare ProtectiveClean 5100?

The Philips Sonicare 5700 (2026) replaces the ProtectiveClean 5100. It uses a new 62,000 movements-per-minute motor, offers three intensity levels, and has a 21-day battery — all improvements over the older model.

Does the Sonicare 5700 have an app?

No. App connectivity begins at the Sonicare 7100 in the 2026 lineup. The 5700 is designed for standalone use.

Is the Sonicare 5700 good for sensitive teeth?

Yes. The dedicated Sensitive mode combined with Level 1 intensity makes the 5700 one of the gentler options in the Sonicare lineup at this price. If sensitivity is your primary concern, the sensitive teeth guide has more specific recommendations.
