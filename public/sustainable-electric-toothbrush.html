Best Sustainable Electric Toothbrushes (2026)

Standard electric toothbrushes create a real waste problem: a replacement head every three months means four plastic heads per year per person, multiplied by the 300+ million electric toothbrush users in the US alone. The good news is that several brands have taken this seriously. Here are the best sustainable options and what actually makes an electric toothbrush eco-friendly.

The Environmental Problem with Standard Electric Toothbrushes

Each replacement head is mostly non-recyclable plastic mixed with nylon bristles. When you put an Oral-B CrossAction head or Sonicare C3 head in the recycling bin, it typically ends up in landfill anyway because recycling facilities cannot separate the mixed materials. Multiply that by four heads per year per person and you have a significant waste stream.

The handles themselves are another concern. When your brush handle finally dies after 3–5 years, it goes to landfill too. The NiMH or Li-ion battery inside should technically go to battery recycling, but few people take that step.

What Actually Makes an Electric Toothbrush Sustainable

Four things matter: (1) recyclable brush heads, whether through the brand's own program or a third-party like TerraCycle; (2) sustainable materials in the handle (recycled plastic, aluminum, or bamboo); (3) long battery life that reduces charging frequency; and (4) minimal, recyclable packaging. No electric toothbrush is truly zero-waste, but some are dramatically better than others on these dimensions.

SURI: The Best Sustainable Pick

The SURI Sonic Toothbrush (~$79) is the clearest sustainability-first option currently available. The handle is made from recycled aluminum and recycled ocean-bound plastic. The brush heads are 96% recyclable, with castor bean oil-derived Rilsan bristles replacing standard nylon. The 40-day battery life means far less frequent charging than daily-charge models. SURI also uses minimal, fully recyclable cardboard packaging with no plastic inserts.

The cleaning performance matches mid-range sonic brushes. SURI is not going to out-clean a Sonicare 9900, but it cleans teeth thoroughly and effectively. For someone whose primary concern is environmental impact without sacrificing effective dental hygiene, SURI is the right choice.

Oral-B TerraCycle Program: The Easiest Upgrade

If you already own an Oral-B brush or prefer their oscillating cleaning action, the Oral-B + TerraCycle recycling program is the easiest sustainability upgrade. You collect your used Oral-B brush heads, request a free prepaid shipping label from TerraCycle's website, and mail them in. TerraCycle then processes the heads into recycled material for new products.

This does not solve the virgin plastic problem of head production, but it diverts heads from landfill. Oral-B iO heads work with this program, so you can use Oral-B's best cleaning technology while keeping heads out of landfill. It requires remembering to participate, which is the main weakness.

Sonicare Recycling Options

Philips Sonicare offers recycling at select retail locations and through mail-in programs in some markets. Availability is more limited than Oral-B's TerraCycle partnership. Check the Sonicare website for current options in your region. Sonicare heads are not currently recyclable through standard curbside programs.

Bamboo Handle Options: WooBamboo and Others

WooBamboo and similar brands make electric toothbrush handles from bamboo, which is biodegradable and rapidly renewable. These are typically compatible with standard oscillating or sonic replacement mechanisms. The bamboo handle reduces the environmental impact of handle production and end-of-life disposal. Cleaning performance is generally equivalent to entry-level name-brand brushes.

Electric vs Manual: Which Is Greener Overall?

A standard manual toothbrush should be replaced every 3 months, generating 4 plastic brushes per year. A standard electric toothbrush generates 4 replacement heads per year plus the handle (lasting 3–5 years). The raw plastic volume is similar. The advantage of sustainable electric options like SURI or the Oral-B TerraCycle program is that they break the direct-to-landfill pipeline that most manual brushes follow.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are electric toothbrushes bad for the environment?

Conventional electric toothbrushes generate more plastic waste per year than manual brushes because of the plastic replacement heads. However, some brands have addressed this: Oral-B partners with TerraCycle for head recycling, Sonicare has recycling programs at select retailers, and SURI makes heads from 96% recyclable materials. A sustainable electric toothbrush with a recycling program can have a lower net environmental impact than a manual toothbrush replaced every 3 months.

What is the most eco-friendly electric toothbrush?

SURI is currently the most eco-friendly electric toothbrush available. Its brush heads are made from 96% recyclable materials including castor bean-based Rilsan bristles, the handle is built from recycled aluminum, and the brand uses minimal, recyclable packaging. The 40-day battery also reduces charging energy compared to brushes charged daily.

Does Oral-B have a recycling program for brush heads?

Yes. Oral-B partners with TerraCycle to offer a free recycling program for Oral-B electric toothbrush heads. You collect used heads, request a prepaid shipping label from TerraCycle, and mail them in. The heads are then recycled into new products. This is available in the US and several other countries.
