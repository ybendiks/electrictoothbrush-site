The Short Answer

Standard electric toothbrushes are not FSA/HSA eligible by default — the IRS classifies them as general health items, not medical treatments. However, electric toothbrushes prescribed by a dentist or physician for a specific medical condition (gum disease, post-surgery recovery, disability) may qualify. Some retailers also list electric toothbrushes as FSA-eligible with documentation requirements.

When Electric Toothbrushes Are HSA/FSA Eligible

The CARES Act (2020) expanded HSA/FSA eligibility to include more over-the-counter items, but electric toothbrushes were not part of that expansion. To use FSA/HSA funds for an electric toothbrush, one of the following typically applies:

  • Letter of Medical Necessity (LMN): A dentist or physician writes a letter stating the brush is required to treat a specific diagnosed condition (periodontitis, post-implant care, arthritic dexterity limitations, etc.)
  • FSA Store / specific retailers: Some FSA-partnered stores list electric toothbrushes as eligible — these eligibility determinations vary by plan administrator
  • Employer plan flexibility: Some employer FSA plans have broader eligibility than the IRS minimum — check your plan documents

How to Get a Dentist's LMN

If you have a diagnosed periodontal condition, dry mouth caused by medication, post-implant recovery needs, or a mobility limitation like arthritis, ask your dentist to write a Letter of Medical Necessity. Most dentists are familiar with this process. The letter should specify the condition, why an electric toothbrush is medically necessary, and the recommended product type.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I use my FSA card to buy an electric toothbrush?

Generally no, without a Letter of Medical Necessity or your plan administrator's specific approval. Some FSA-partnered stores list electric toothbrushes as eligible — check with your plan documents and the retailer before purchasing.

Are replacement brush heads FSA eligible?

Same rules apply as the brush itself. Without a qualifying medical condition or LMN, replacement heads are typically not FSA-eligible as general dental hygiene products.

What dental products are FSA eligible?

Confirmed FSA-eligible dental items include: prescription mouth rinses, fluoride treatments, denture adhesives, teeth-grinding night guards (with Rx), dental procedures, and orthodontic treatment. Standard toothbrushes and toothpaste are generally not eligible without a prescription or LMN.
